PC Turns On but No Display? 5 Fixes to Try Before Calling a Technician

By Aizen · October 15, 2026 · 4 min read

You press the power button, the fans spin and the lights turn on, but the screen stays black. This is a very common problem, and the cause is often something simple like a loose cable or a memory stick that has shifted.

Work through the checks below in order. Always turn the computer off and unplug it before touching anything inside the case.

Quick checks first (2 minutes)

Before you open anything:

  • Make sure the monitor is powered on and the power light is lit
  • Check the monitor's input source (HDMI, DisplayPort, VGA, DVI) matches the cable you use
  • Press a key and move the mouse in case the PC is in sleep mode
  • Look for any beeps or blinking lights on the case, which can point to the problem

Fix 1: Check and reseat the video cable

  1. Turn everything off.
  2. Unplug the video cable at both ends and plug it back in firmly.
  3. Try a different cable if you have one. Cables fail more often than people expect.
  4. If your monitor has another input, try that one.
  5. Test with a different monitor or a TV if possible. This tells you whether the monitor or the computer is at fault.

Fix 2: Use the correct video port

If your desktop has a dedicated graphics card (a card with its own fan, installed in a slot at the back), the cable must go into the graphics card, not the motherboard's built-in port. A cable plugged into the wrong port gives a black screen even though the PC is running.

If you do not have a graphics card, use the motherboard's video port.

Fix 3: Disconnect extra devices

Sometimes a faulty USB device or a second screen stops the PC from starting properly.

  1. Turn off the PC and unplug everything except the power, the monitor and the keyboard.
  2. Turn it on and see if the display appears.
  3. If it does, plug the other devices back in one at a time to find the faulty one.

Fix 4: Reseat the RAM (memory)

Loose memory is one of the top causes of a no-display problem.

  1. Turn off the PC, unplug the power cable and press the power button once to drain leftover power.
  2. Open the side panel.
  3. Touch a metal part of the case to discharge static electricity.
  4. Press down the clips on each side of a memory stick, remove it and push it back until the clips click.
  5. If you have more than one stick, try one stick at a time in the first slot. A faulty stick can be the entire problem.
  6. Close the case, reconnect the power and turn on.

Fix 5: Reset the BIOS (clear CMOS)

A wrong BIOS setting after a power cut or a hardware change can stop the display.

  1. Turn off and unplug the PC.
  2. Find the round, silver coin battery on the motherboard.
  3. Remove it, wait about 5 minutes, then put it back in the same direction.
  4. Plug in the power and start the PC. The BIOS will use its default settings.

Many motherboards also have a Clear CMOS jumper or button. Check the motherboard manual for the exact location.

For laptops

  • Hold the power button for about 30 seconds with the charger unplugged to do a hard reset, then try again
  • Remove the battery if it is removable, hold the power button for 30 seconds, then reconnect everything
  • Connect an external monitor. If the external screen works, the laptop's screen or its cable is faulty, not the whole computer

What the signs may mean

What you notice Possible cause
Beeps at startup RAM or graphics problem (check your motherboard manual for the beep code)
Fans run, no beeps, no display CPU, motherboard or power supply problem
Display works with another monitor Original monitor or cable is faulty
Works with one RAM stick only The other stick or slot is faulty

When to call a technician

If you have tried all five fixes and tested with another monitor and cable, the cause may be the graphics card, power supply, motherboard or processor. These are harder to diagnose without spare parts to swap. Ask for a diagnosis quote before you agree to a repair, and ask for a written estimate.

Tips to avoid it next time

  • Use a surge protector or UPS to avoid power problems
  • Clean dust from the inside of a desktop every 6 to 12 months
  • Do not force cables into ports
  • Handle RAM and cards by their edges
